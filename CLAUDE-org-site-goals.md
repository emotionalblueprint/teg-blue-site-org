# TEG-Blue .org Site — Goals & Writing Principles

## Site Identity

**teg-blue.org** is the academic and practitioner-facing platform for TEG-Blue research.
Its audience is researchers, clinicians, and serious practitioners — not the general public.
The general public platform is teg-blue.com.

---

## Site Goals (in priority order)

### 1. Build academic credibility and research partnerships
The site must demonstrate that TEG-Blue knows the literature it synthesizes — precisely, accurately, and with appropriate humility about what is established versus what is proposed. A researcher landing on any page must see their own field handled correctly before they will engage with the contribution. Credibility is earned by precision, not volume.

The site must also make falsifiable claims visible. What does TEG-Blue predict that existing frameworks don't? Testable propositions should be explicit — not buried in framework text — so the site functions as a research invitation, not just a presentation.

### 2. Convert practitioners into users of the framework
The site must answer one question above all others for practitioners: **does this change how I work tomorrow?** Not in five years after reading all twelve frameworks. Tomorrow.

The entry point for practitioners is always a problem they already have — the client who understands everything and changes nothing, the chronic mode that looks like personality, the insight that produces no movement. The site must show it has a precise account of exactly those situations. That is what converts a practitioner from reader to user.

### 3. Establish TEG-Blue as a citable theoretical contribution
This goal cannot be pursued directly. It is earned by doing goals 1 and 2 well enough that the field begins citing the framework independently. The site creates the conditions for this — it does not claim it.

---

## The Governing Writing Principle: Anchor — Gap — Contribution

Every section of every page on teg-blue.org follows this three-part structure:

**Anchor:** Open by naming what the relevant research tradition already established — precisely, with correct attribution, with genuine respect for the contribution. The reader must recognize their own field being handled accurately before they will trust what comes next.

**Gap:** Name explicitly where that research stopped. Not as a criticism — as a factual observation about the question that tradition was asking versus the question TEG-Blue is asking. The gap must be specific, not general.

**Contribution:** Show what TEG-Blue does from exactly that stopping point. Not competing with the existing science — picking up where it left off. The contribution is defined by the gap it fills.

**Example from M3:**
- Anchor: Sapolsky, LeDoux, Porges documented the biology of threat activation with extraordinary precision.
- Gap: That literature dissolves shame, grief, rage, and longing into undifferentiated "stress activation." The felt named emotion disappears into the mechanism.
- Contribution: M3 connects the specific felt emotion to the specific biological cascade — holding both as the same event described from two angles. For the first time, the biology and the felt experience are mapped together.

This structure builds credibility with researchers (they see their literature handled correctly) and recognition with practitioners (they see their clinical experience named precisely) — simultaneously, from the same text.

---

## What the Site Should Not Do

- **Do not convince through volume.** More frameworks, more citations, more content does not build credibility. Precision does. One page that handles the literature impeccably is worth more than twelve pages that handle it approximately.
- **Do not front-run legitimacy.** Do not claim TEG-Blue is an established contribution. Demonstrate the work. Let the field decide.
- **Do not write for the general public.** teg-blue.com exists for that. The .org site should assume a reader who knows the literature and will notice if it is handled incorrectly.
- **Do not bury the testable claims.** Every framework and model page should make explicit what TEG-Blue predicts that existing frameworks do not predict — and where those predictions can be tested.

## The Three Models — Positioning

**M1 — The Inner Compass & Four-Mode Gradient** (The Instrument)
Answers: *Where is the needle, can it move, and what does the person have access to from where they are?*

**M2 — Three Awareness Capacities** (The Calibration System)
Answers: *Why is the needle where it is, and what would it take to move it?*

**M3 — The Open Cycle** (The Biology of Unfinished Emotion)
Answers: *What is the body doing beneath the compass, and what does it cost when that process is never allowed to finish?*

The three models are three dimensions of the same reality. Complete understanding requires all three.

## The Origin of the Framework

TEG-Blue was not designed top-down. It emerged by following the evidence one layer at a time.

The Inner Compass began as an attempt to make Polyvagal Theory visually navigable. Explaining Connection and Protection required more context than a model could hold — that context became F1. Each subsequent framework followed the research tradition that explained the next layer: IFS and Winnicott shaped F2; cognitive dissonance, defense mechanisms, and Goffman shaped F3. When the twelve frameworks were placed together, the gradient became visible. It was not constructed. It emerged.

This origin matters for how the site presents the work: not as a system someone built, but as a structure that became visible when enough existing research traditions were placed in conversation with each other.

## Tone

- Rigorous without being inaccessible
- Precise without being cold
- Confident about the contribution without overclaiming
- Always anchored in existing science before extending it
- Never promotional — the work speaks, the site does not sell
