## Systemic Findings

### Finding 1: The Gap Is Structurally Absent Across the Entire Site

The governing writing principle requires every section to follow **Anchor → Gap → Contribution**. In practice, the site consistently delivers **Anchor → Contribution**, skipping the gap. Of ~150+ content sections audited across all pages, fewer than 10 explicitly name where existing research stopped.

**Best examples of gap naming (use as templates):**
- M3 Concept 7 (Open Cycle and the Gradient): "The existing stress physiology literature — Sapolsky, McEwen, Porges — describes physiological states without a gradient model connecting them to each other... The gap between them is exactly the space M3 and the TEG-Blue gradient occupy together."
- F4 Seven-Step Mechanism: "to our knowledge, no existing model traces the complete pathway from individual threat activation through collective rule invisibility..."
- F6 Bias as Regulation: "to our knowledge, no existing framework unifies them under a single formulation that names the regulatory function as primary."

**Recommendation:** FLAG FOR HUMAN REVIEW — Each framework/model page needs explicit gap sentences added to every section. Suggest format: "What [tradition] did not do was..." or "Where this knowledge stopped was..." This is a significant content task.

### Finding 2: Citation Precision Degrades from Models to Later Frameworks

| Tier | Pattern |
|------|---------|
| **M3, F1-F2** | Named researchers with years and specific contributions |
| **F3-F7** | Named researchers, many missing years, some generic tradition labels |
| **F8-F12** | Named researchers only (no years, no works, no specific claims) |
| **Epistemological Foundations, Emotional Technology** | Zero citations |

**Recommendation:** FLAG FOR HUMAN REVIEW — F8-F12 need citation precision brought up to M3/F1-F2 standard. Epistemological Foundations and Emotional Technology need complete research anchoring.

### Finding 3: Falsifiable Predictions Present on Only 7 of 31 Pages

**Pages with explicit predictions:** Research Entry, M1, M3, F4, F6, Frameworks Map, AI Safety

**Pages where predictions should exist but don't:** F1, F2, F3, F5, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, F12, M2, Scientific Foundations

**Recommendation:** FLAG FOR HUMAN REVIEW — Each framework page should contain at least one explicit falsifiable prediction. F6's "Testable Prediction" box is the best template.

### Finding 4: "The First Complete Emotional Technology System" — Recurring Overclaim

This phrase appears on: Home page, About page, Emotional Technology page, AI Safety page.

It is a priority claim ("first") and completeness claim ("complete") that is never anchored. No prior system is named. No definition of "complete" is given. No gap is established.

**Recommendation:** FLAG FOR HUMAN REVIEW — Either establish what makes this claim valid (name prior systems, define "complete") or soften to "a comprehensive emotional technology system" or similar.

### Finding 5: Two Pages Need Fundamental Rework

1. **Epistemological Foundations** — Makes substantive philosophical claims about emotion-as-data without citing any philosopher or scientist (Damasio, Nussbaum, de Sousa, Barrett, Prinz). Non-compliant on all 4 criteria. This is a credibility risk on a research site.

2. **Emotional Technology** — Defines a new field without referencing prior work in affective computing, emotion measurement, or emotional intelligence. Non-compliant on all 4 criteria. Reads as marketing rather than scholarship.

**Recommendation:** FLAG FOR HUMAN REVIEW — Both pages marked for rewrite. Do not rewrite without human approval and source material.
