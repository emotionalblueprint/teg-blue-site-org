## How TEG-Blue Is Organized

TEG-Blue has two layers. The distinction between them is central to understanding the architecture and identifying where specific research contributions connect.

### The Measurement System: The Four-Mode Gradient

The Four-Mode Gradient is the measurable backbone of TEG-Blue. It proposes that emotional regulation operates on a gradient with four observable positions:

**Connection → Protection → Control → Domination**

These are not personality types or diagnostic categories. They are **nervous system states** — regulatory positions that shift in response to perceived threat, shaped by attachment history, social context, and current capacity. They can be detected in natural language. They have been empirically validated. They are where research begins.

### The Explanatory Architecture: 12 Frameworks

Behind the gradient sit 12 interconnected frameworks that explain *why* these four modes exist, *how* individual regulation patterns scale into social structures, *where* protection tips into domination, and *what* makes change possible. These frameworks integrate 145+ established theories across neuroscience, psychology, sociology, and trauma studies.

The measurement system (the gradient) can be engaged with independently of the full explanatory architecture. The frameworks provide the theoretical depth — explaining why escalation follows the patterns it does, how social systems reproduce regulatory states across generations, and what makes change possible. Research can operate at either level, or bridge both.

### The Core Testable Claim

The central proposition that connects both layers is this:

> **The key variable that predicts relational and behavioral outcomes is not a person's current regulatory state, but their *capacity to return to baseline when challenged.***

This capacity is measurable. It shows up in language. And it appears to be predicted by specific linguistic markers we call **complexity markers** — signs of self-awareness, perspective-taking, and emotional differentiation that appear in natural discourse.

## What Is Original in TEG-Blue

TEG-Blue is built on established science. Every source theory is credited, and the theoretical foundations page names 135+ researchers whose work informs the architecture. The originality is not in the individual theories. It is in how they are connected, and in what those connections reveal.

### What TEG-Blue uses (established, credited)

The measurement system and explanatory architecture draw on well-established research traditions:

- **Polyvagal Theory** (Porges) — nervous system states as regulatory responses to perceived safety and threat
- **Attachment Theory** (Bowlby, Ainsworth, Main) — early relational patterns as templates for regulation
- **Moral Disengagement** (Bandura) — cognitive mechanisms that allow harmful behavior
- **Social Dominance Theory** (Sidanius, Pratto) — how hierarchies reproduce through psychological mechanisms
- **Cognitive Bias Research** (Kahneman, Tversky) — systematic distortions in perception and judgment
- **Conflict Escalation Models** (Glasl, Gottman) — how relational rupture progresses through stages

These and 130+ additional theories are documented with full attribution at [teg-blue.org/theoretical-foundations](https://teg-blue.org/theoretical-foundations).

### What TEG-Blue connects (the integrative architecture)

These research traditions developed independently, within separate disciplines, often without reference to each other. TEG-Blue proposes specific cross-disciplinary connections that do not exist in any of the source theories:

- **Nervous system regulation → moral perception.** Polyvagal Theory describes autonomic states. Moral psychology describes how people make ethical judgments. TEG-Blue proposes that regulatory state systematically shapes which moral judgments a person makes — that perception is state-dependent, not just cognitively biased.

- **Attachment patterns → social stratification.** Attachment theory operates at the individual and relational scale. Sociology of capital (Bourdieu) operates at the social scale. TEG-Blue proposes that the same protective mechanisms that organize individual identity also organize social hierarchies — that worth-sorting is threat-based rule internalization operating at scale.

- **Self-protection → domination as a continuous gradient.** Clinical psychology studies narcissism, coercive control, and moral disengagement as separate phenomena. Polyvagal Theory studies defensive states. TEG-Blue maps these onto a single continuous trajectory — from legitimate self-protection through ego protection through control to domination — with identifiable transition markers at each stage.

- **Linguistic complexity → regulatory capacity.** Psycholinguistics has studied language as a window into cognitive states. Emotion regulation research has studied the capacity to return to baseline. TEG-Blue connects these by proposing that specific linguistic markers (complexity markers) are measurable indicators of the capacity to return to Connection — a construct that neither field has operationalized.

No single source theory makes any of these connections. They become visible only when the theories are placed in relation to each other.

### What TEG-Blue generates (new questions and findings)

The connections above are not just theoretical propositions. They generate specific, testable questions that could not emerge from any of the source disciplines alone:

- *Does nervous system regulatory state predict moral judgment patterns?* — This question requires connecting polyvagal theory with moral psychology. Neither field has asked it.
- *Are there measurable linguistic markers that predict de-escalation capacity?* — This question requires connecting psycholinguistics with emotion regulation research. The validation study found evidence that the answer is yes (78% higher complexity marker rates in de-escalators).
- *Does the pathway from self-protection to domination follow a predictable linguistic sequence?* — This question requires connecting clinical research on coercive control with discourse analysis and polyvagal theory.
- *Do the same regulatory mechanisms that shape individual identity also shape social hierarchies?* — This question requires connecting attachment theory with sociology of capital at a mechanistic level.

The validation study (described below) tested several of these questions and produced empirical findings — including the four-mode detection, the escalation/de-escalation rates, and the complexity marker differential — that did not exist in the literature before TEG-Blue.

The originality, in summary, is the architecture itself: the specific connections between established theories, the testable propositions those connections generate, and the empirical findings that have begun to emerge from testing them.

## What Has Already Been Demonstrated

### Validation Study (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18428907)

A computational analysis of 10,000+ natural conflict narratives (Reddit AITA posts) tested whether the four-mode gradient could be reliably detected in unstructured text.

**Key findings:**

- All four regulatory modes were successfully detected using polyvagal markers, contempt markers, and moral disengagement markers
- **33.8% of individuals escalated** toward Control/Domination when challenged by commenters
- **22.2% de-escalated** toward Connection
- De-escalators showed **78% higher rates of complexity markers** than escalators
- Mode classifications correlated with independent community moral judgments (external validation)

**What this established:**
The four-mode gradient is not just a theoretical model — it maps onto observable patterns in natural language. And the distinction between escalation and de-escalation is linguistically measurable.

### Pre-registration

The study methodology was pre-registered on OSF prior to analysis, establishing methodological credibility.

### Theoretical Architecture Paper

A comprehensive paper documenting the full TEG-Blue architecture (12 interconnected frameworks, theoretical integration across 47+ domains) is deposited on Zenodo, establishing intellectual priority.

## The Open Research Questions

The validation study opened more questions than it answered. Below are specific research questions that remain open. Each stands as an independent research project. Each connects to an established literature. And each can be addressed using existing data, new data collection, or both.

### Question 1: What exactly are the complexity markers, and can they be standardized?

**The gap:** Self-awareness is discussed everywhere in psychology but rarely operationalized as a measurable linguistic construct. There is no validated linguistic measure of "capacity to return to connection."

**What's needed:**
- Catalogue and classify the specific complexity markers identified in the validation dataset
- Determine which markers are most predictive of de-escalation
- Test whether marker combinations outperform individual markers
- Develop a preliminary Complexity Marker Index that independent raters can apply
- Examine whether different modes show different kinds of complexity

**Relevant expertise:** Computational linguistics, NLP, psycholinguistics, quantitative psychology

**Existing literature this extends:** Pennebaker's linguistic markers of psychological states; Tausczik & Pennebaker (2010) on language and social processes; linguistic indicators of cognitive complexity (Suedfeld & Tetlock)

**Related TEG-Blue frameworks:** F1 (Emotional Gradient), F3 (Cognitive Coherence), F6 (State-Dependent Perception)

---

### Question 2: What do escalation and de-escalation pathways look like in natural language?

**The gap:** Escalation models exist in conflict research (Glasl, Gottman), but none map the specific linguistic trajectory from protective responses through to controlling or dominating ones — or the reverse trajectory back toward connection.

**What's needed:**
- Map the sequential language patterns of the 33.8% who escalated — is there a predictable sequence?
- Map the trajectory of the 22.2% who de-escalated — what precedes the turning point?
- Identify linguistic markers of the "crossroads moment" where trajectories diverge
- Test whether the ego protection vs. legitimate protection distinction appears empirically
- Compare observed escalation patterns against TEG-Blue's theorized 9-step sequence

**Relevant expertise:** Conflict studies, discourse analysis, social psychology, clinical psychology

**Existing literature this extends:** Gottman's cascade model of relational deterioration; Glasl's conflict escalation stages; Johnson's coercive control typology; Porges's polyvagal theory of social engagement

**Related TEG-Blue frameworks:** F1 (Emotional Gradient), F7 (Anatomy of Tyranny), F4 (Threat-Based Rule Internalization)

---

### Question 3: Can the four-mode classification be reproduced by independent researchers?

**The gap:** The validation study demonstrated that the four modes can be detected computationally. But for the framework to become a research tool others can build on, the classification methodology needs to be formalized, tested for inter-rater reliability, and published as a reproducible coding instrument.

**What's needed:**
- Formalize the classification criteria (polyvagal markers, contempt markers, moral disengagement markers) into a coding manual
- Test inter-rater reliability with trained coders
- Validate classifications against the external community judgments
- Identify boundary cases and refine criteria
- Publish the coding framework as a downloadable research instrument

**Relevant expertise:** Research methodology, psychometrics, content analysis, behavioral coding

**Existing literature this extends:** Gottman's SPAFF coding system; Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count (LIWC) methodology; behavioral coding traditions in clinical psychology

**Related TEG-Blue frameworks:** F1 (Emotional Gradient), F12 (Two Information Systems)

---

### Question 4: Does regulatory state shape moral perception?

**The gap:** Moral psychology has extensively studied how people make moral judgments (Haidt, Greene, Kohlberg), but has not examined whether a person's current nervous system regulatory state systematically shifts *which* moral judgments they make and *how* they perceive the same conflict.

**What's needed:**
- Compare how individuals in different modes describe similar conflict types
- Test whether mode position predicts moral judgment patterns
- Examine whether regulatory state predicts which "side" someone takes in ambiguous conflicts
- Test whether state-dependent language differences match TEG-Blue's theoretical predictions about the Architecture of Bias

**Relevant expertise:** Moral psychology, social cognition, cognitive bias research, social psychology

**Existing literature this extends:** Haidt's moral foundations theory; Kahneman's cognitive bias research; Jost's system justification theory; Greenwald & Banaji's implicit cognition research

**Related TEG-Blue frameworks:** F5 (Threat-Driven Worth Sorting), F6 (State-Dependent Perception)

---

### Question 5: Can the Emotional Tools be validated as psychometric instruments?

**The gap:** TEG-Blue includes 16 assessment instruments (gradient scales for empathy, accountability, control-care, confidence, entitlement, integrity, and others) that are designed but not yet psychometrically validated.

**What's needed:**
- Recruit participant samples (online platforms such as Prolific, university populations)
- Administer TEG-Blue gradient scales alongside established comparison measures
- Test whether the four-mode gradient structure fits the data better than existing categorical models
- Establish reliability (internal consistency, test-retest) and validity (convergent, discriminant, criterion)

**Priority instruments for first validation:**
- **Empathy Gradient Scale** → compare against Davis's Interpersonal Reactivity Index (IRI)
- **Accountability Gradient Scale** → compare against existing responsibility and locus of control measures

**Relevant expertise:** Psychometrics, clinical psychology, personality assessment, scale development

**Related TEG-Blue frameworks:** All frameworks contribute to tool design; F1 and F8–F10 are most directly tested

## How the 12 Frameworks Fit

The research questions above all test the **measurement system** — the Four-Mode Gradient. The 12 frameworks below are the **explanatory architecture** that sits behind it. They explain where the gradient comes from, why it behaves the way it does, and how it operates at different scales.

Each research question connects to specific frameworks, but the full architecture is not required for any single line of inquiry. The frameworks organize as follows:

| Layer | Frameworks | What They Explain |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Formation** | F1 (Emotional Gradient), F2 (Identity as Adaptive System), F3 (Cognitive Coherence) | How nervous system states form and how identity organizes around them |
| **Scaling** | F4 (Rule Internalization), F5 (Worth Sorting), F6 (State-Dependent Perception) | How individual regulation patterns become social structures |
| **Turning Point** | F7 (Anatomy of Tyranny) | How protection escalates into domination — and where intervention is possible |
| **Healing** | F8 (Self-Reconnection), F9 (Nervous System Variation), F10 (Generational Transmission) | How patterns shift, including neurodivergent pathways and intergenerational dynamics |
| **Integration** | F11 (Emotional Logic of Paradoxes), F12 (Two Information Systems) | The complete architecture and its internal logic |

The full theoretical mapping — including 145+ source theories across 47 domains — is documented at [teg-blue.org/theoretical-foundations](https://teg-blue.org/theoretical-foundations). Each framework connects to established research traditions in neuroscience, psychology, sociology, and trauma studies.
