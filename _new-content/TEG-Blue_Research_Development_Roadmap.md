## Phase 2: Extract From Existing Data (March–August 2026)

These papers all come from your *existing* 10,000+ post dataset. No new data collection required. Each isolates a specific finding that stands on its own.

### Paper 1: The Complexity Markers Paper
**Working title:** *"Linguistic Markers of Self-Awareness in Conflict: What Predicts De-escalation vs. Escalation?"*

**The question:** What *exactly* are the linguistic features that distinguish people who de-escalate from those who escalate when challenged?

**Why this matters to the field:** There is no existing validated linguistic measure of "capacity to return to connection." Self-awareness is discussed everywhere in psychology but rarely operationalized in natural language. This paper would fill that gap.

**What to extract:**
- Catalogue the specific complexity markers identified
- Statistical analysis of which markers are most predictive
- Create a preliminary "Complexity Marker Index"
- Test whether marker combinations are more predictive than individual markers
- Examine whether markers differ by mode (do Connection and Protection show different kinds of complexity?)

**Target venues:** 
- Zenodo first (establish priority)
- Then submit to: *Journal of Language and Social Psychology*, *Cognition and Emotion*, or *Behavior Research Methods*

**This is your strongest next paper.** It turns a descriptive finding into a measurable construct.

### Paper 2: The Escalation Pathway Paper  
**Working title:** *"From Protection to Domination: Mapping Escalation and De-escalation Trajectories in Natural Conflict Discourse"*

**The question:** What does the pathway from Protection → Control → Domination actually look like in real language? And what does de-escalation look like?

**Why this matters:** Your framework proposes a specific escalation sequence (the Crossroads concept, the fork point). Your data can test whether this sequence appears in natural discourse, and whether the transition markers you've theorized (from Map Level 7 and the Deep Diver framework) actually show up.

**What to extract:**
- Map the linguistic trajectory of the 33.8% who escalated — is there a sequence?
- Map the trajectory of the 22.2% who de-escalated — what precedes the turn?
- Identify transition markers: what language signals the "crossroads moment"?
- Test the ego protection vs. legitimate protection distinction empirically
- Examine whether the 9-step escalation sequence from your Deep Diver maps onto observed patterns

**Target venues:**  
- *Aggressive Behavior*, *Journal of Interpersonal Violence*, or *Conflict Resolution Quarterly*

---

### Paper 3: The Mode Detection Methodology Paper  
**Working title:** *"Detecting Regulatory States in Text: A Natural Language Framework for Four-Mode Classification"*

**The question:** Can the TEG-Blue 4-mode classification be reliably applied to natural language, and how?

**Why this matters:** If your mode detection methodology is reproducible, other researchers can use it. This turns your framework into a *research tool* that other people cite and build on. Methodology papers get cited heavily.

**What to extract:**
- Formalize the classification criteria used (polyvagal markers, contempt markers, moral disengagement markers)
- Report inter-rater reliability if available, or propose a coding manual
- Test classification against the community judgments (your external validation)
- Discuss limitations and boundary cases
- Provide the coding framework as a downloadable instrument

**Target venues:**  
- *Behavior Research Methods*, *Assessment*, or deposit as a research tool on Zenodo/OSF

### Paper 4: The State-Dependent Perception Paper  
**Working title:** *"Same Conflict, Different Reality: How Regulatory State Shapes Moral Judgment in Online Discourse"*

**The question:** Do people in different modes perceive and judge the same conflict situations differently — and can you see this in their language?

**Why this matters:** This directly tests Map Level 6 (Architecture of Bias) and the concept of state-dependent perception. It connects your work to the massive literature on moral psychology (Haidt, Greene) and cognitive bias research.

**What to extract:**
- Compare how individuals in different modes describe similar conflict types
- Examine whether mode predicts moral judgment pattern
- Test whether mode position predicts which "side" someone takes
- Look at whether state-dependent language differences match your theoretical predictions

**Target venues:**  
- *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, *Cognition and Emotion*, *Social Psychological and Personality Science*

## Phase 3: Validate the Instruments (September 2026 – 2027)

These require new data collection but build directly on the credibility established by Phases 1–2.

### Paper 5: Tool Validation Studies
Your 11 Emotional Tools are assessment instruments waiting to be validated. Each gradient scale (Empathy, Accountability, Control-Care, Confidence, Entitlement, Integrity) could be validated as a psychometric instrument.

**Approach:** 
- Start with 1–2 tools (Empathy Gradient and Accountability Scale are strongest candidates)
- Recruit participants online (Prolific, university samples)
- Correlate with established measures (e.g., Empathy Gradient against Davis's IRI; Accountability Scale against existing responsibility measures)
- Test whether your gradient structure (Connection → Protection → Control → Domination) fits the data better than existing categorical models

### Paper 6: The Circuit Board Validation
The Circuit Board concept — that individual gradient position can be calculated from societal friction + childhood conditioning + current state — is a testable proposition. This could become a major assessment instrument paper if the model holds.
